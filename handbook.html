If the feed goes quiet, the price comes off — on purpose

The reader that watches Big River's board checks in every six hours. If nothing has arrived in 14 hours — two missed check-ins — both sites stop showing a number and say “Call for today's price” instead.

That is the system working. A wrong price on the page is worse than no price on the page.

Mail to the two elevator names

Kristi · to be set up

A farmer who has the website will sooner or later write to info@badgergrain.com, because that is the address people assume exists. Right now that note bounces — or worse, quietly does not. The fix is forwarding: mail addressed to either elevator lands in Kristi's inbox at midwestagsupply.com, which is where the office already reads its mail.

Set up the forwarding

  1. Sign in at godaddy.com — both elevator names are registered there.
  2. Open badgergrain.com and find the email forwarding section for that domain.
  3. Forward info@badgergrain.com to kristi@midwestagsupply.com.
  4. Add a second forward for office@ if you want it. Keep the list short — every address you invent is one more thing to check.
  5. Do the same for midwestcommodity.com.
  6. Send yourself a test to each new address. It can take an hour or two to start working; if nothing has arrived by the next morning, something is wrong.

Then put the address on the sites

  1. Tell Sig once the forwarding is tested and working.
  2. An address on a website that bounces is worse than no address at all, so the sites do not get one until a real note has arrived.
  3. The phone number stays the main way to reach either elevator. This is for the people who write instead of ring.

Two records that stop somebody pretending to be us

While the domain is open in GoDaddy, add these. They are the reason a fraudster cannot send a convincing “our bank details have changed” note that appears to come from the elevator — which is a known and specific way grain businesses get hit.

Add, never replace. Adding a record is safe; replacing one that is already there is how mail goes down.

On each of the two elevator domains, add two TXT records:

Type   Name      Value
────   ──────    ─────────────────────────────
TXT    @         v=spf1 -all
TXT    _dmarc    v=DMARC1; p=reject;

What those two lines mean, and the one way they could bite

The first says nothing on the internet is allowed to send mail claiming to be this name. The second says and if anything tries, throw it away. Forwarding is unaffected: forwarding is mail coming in, and these two records only govern mail going out.

The one way they could bite: if the office ever starts sending mail as kristi@badgergrain.com rather than just receiving it, these records will block your own mail. That is fixable in a minute — but it has to be done at the same time, not discovered afterwards. Tell Sig before that changes.

Why a ticket and not a password

There used to be a password on this screen, kept in a box on another company's service, guarding a button that changed two small files. That was removed in August 2026.

A ticket is better for a plain reason: a password says what you know, an account says who you are. Every change now carries your name and the time, nothing has to be remembered or shared, and there is no password to leak, expire or be written on a note by the till.

The price reader, in detail

the part nobody has to run

This is the piece people ask about most, so here it is properly. Nobody in the office runs it, starts it, or can break it — but knowing what it does answers most of the questions a farmer will put to you.

What it reads

  1. Big River Resources posts a cash-bid board on their own website, publicly, the same board any farmer can look at.
  2. Every ten minutes through the trading day, a job opens that page and reads the whole table.
  3. It keeps one copy. Both elevators price off the same copy, which is why the two columns on the staff screen can be checked against each other.

What it refuses

  1. A reading where their cash and their basis do not agree with the futures quote on their own page — that is a half-loaded page, not a price.
  2. A corn price outside two to twelve dollars. That is a decimal point in the wrong place, not a market.
  3. Anything older than 14 hours. Both sites then say “Call for today's price” rather than show it.

What it never does

  1. It never invents a number. If it cannot read one, the price comes off the page.
  2. It never rounds Big River's figures. $4.2825 stays $4.2825 so you can check it line for line.
  3. It never sets our price. Our price is their board minus the spread you set, worked out by each site itself.

So if a farmer asks “where does this price come from?”

The honest answer, and the one on the page: it is Big River's posted board less our basis, read automatically and rebuilt through the day. It is not a quote, and it is not final until he rings.

How to

the four things you will actually do

Change the hours

  1. Open the staff screen.
  2. Find the elevator's column — Badger on the left, Midwest on the right.
  3. Type the new times. The fields you changed light up before you save.
  4. Press Save. A GitHub page opens with the form already filled in.
  5. Press Submit new issue. That is the actual save.

Close for the day

  1. Tick Closed today in that elevator's column.
  2. Add a line in the notice box if there is a reason worth giving.
  3. Save and submit as above.
  4. It clears itself overnight — you do not have to remember to untick it tomorrow.

Change the basis

  1. The board on the screen shows Big River's posted numbers, line for line, exactly as they post them.
  2. Our basis is their basis minus our spread. Change the spread and ours moves with theirs from then on.
  3. The readout under the box says in words what the number you typed will do.
  4. Save and submit. The price on the site is rebuilt within a minute.

Break glass: type a price

  1. Only when the automatic reading is down and you have a number from the office.
  2. Enter it as a manual price. It overrides the feed until it is cleared.
  3. Clear it the moment the feed is back, or the site keeps showing your number instead of the market's.

The one that catches people: blank is not zero

On the new-crop spread box, blank and 0 mean opposite things.

Blank — there is no separate new-crop spread, so new crop uses the same spread as everything else.
0 — there is a new-crop spread and it is zero: we pay Big River's exact posted board, no cents off.

Read the sentence under the box before you save. It spells out what will happen in plain words.

What Save actually does

why there are two buttons

There is no password on the staff screen and no server behind it. That is deliberate — the password and the server were removed in August 2026 because a password kept in a box somewhere is the thing that eventually leaks. Instead the screen writes the change as a GitHub ticket, and GitHub decides whether you are allowed.

StepWhat happensWhat you see
1You press Save on the screen.A GitHub page opens with the whole form already written out.
2You press Submit new issue.The ticket is filed against that elevator's site.
3A checker runs. It reads who filed it and refuses anyone without write access; it runs its own tests before it touches a file.Nothing yet — about a minute.
4aAccepted. The hours and spread are written, the site is rebuilt straight away.A comment saying Done, and the ticket closes.
4bRefused — a time that could not be read, a basis that is not a number. Nothing is changed.A comment saying what was wrong, and the ticket stays open.

A closed ticket is the receipt

The ticket closing is how you know the change is live. If it is still open, read the comment — it names the line that could not be read.

You do not start over. Edit the ticket, fix that one line, and it runs again on its own.

Questions

open one
I saved it. How long until it is on the site?

About a minute. Saving now kicks off the rebuild rather than waiting for the next scheduled run.

If it has been more than five minutes, check whether the ticket closed. An open ticket means it was refused and nothing changed.

The site says “Call for today's price”. Is it broken?

Not necessarily. It means no fresh reading of Big River's board has arrived in 14 hours, so the site took the number down rather than show a stale one.

It clears itself as soon as a reading arrives. If it is still saying that after a few hours of a normal trading day, tell Sig.

The hours box says Closed and we are open.

The open/closed sentence is worked out in the visitor's own browser, from their own clock, so it flips at the right minute without anything having to run. If it is wrong, the hours themselves are wrong, not the clock — check what is set on the staff screen for that elevator.

Worth ruling out first: a phone showing a page it loaded hours ago. Pull to refresh.

I typed the wrong thing and saved it.

Save the right thing over it. Every save is a full statement of what the hours and spread should be — the last one wins, and the site rebuilds within a minute.

Nothing is lost either way: every change is recorded with who made it and when.

Why does the board on the staff screen show $4.2825 and not $4.28?

Because that is what Big River posted. The board is there so you can check it line for line against theirs, and a rounded number would not match.

The rounding that produces our posted price happens later, in one place, on purpose.

Can I edit the website itself from the staff screen?

No, and that is the point. The screen sets hours, notices and the spread. Everything else on the page — the price, the week table, the drying schedule — is worked out from those.

Does the website slow down or cost money when a lot of people visit?

No. Both sites are plain HTML files served by GitHub. The page does no work when somebody opens it — it fetches nothing, calculates nothing, asks no other service for anything. A busy morning costs exactly the same as a quiet one.

A farmer says our price is “as of” hours ago. Is it stale?

Almost always no. Two different times are involved and only one of them is on the page.

The board is read every ten minutes through the trading day — hourly outside it, every three hours at weekends. The copy of it is only re-stamped when a number actually moves, or once every six hours if nothing has. So on a flat afternoon the stamp can read six hours old while the board has in fact been read dozens of times since.

What it means is “this is still the price”, not “nobody has looked”. If it goes past 14 hours the site takes the price down on its own.

Somebody shared the site and the preview looked wrong.

Each site has its own preview card, in its own colour — Badger red, Midwest green. Facebook and Messages cache these for days, so an old share can keep showing an old card for a while. A fresh share will be right.

Later: analytics and search

not set up · here is how

None of this is on the sites today. When you want it, it is a short job — and it is safe to add, because the jobs that rebuild the pages only rewrite five clearly marked blocks and never touch the top of the file. That was tested, not assumed: tags were added to a real page, both rebuilders were run against it, and the top of the file came back byte for byte identical.

Google Search Console

  1. Add a URL-prefix property for https://badgergrain.com/. Do the same again for Midwest.
  2. Choose the HTML tag method and copy the tag it gives you.
  3. Paste it into index.html just above </head> and commit.
  4. Press Verify.
  5. Submit https://badgergrain.com/sitemap.xml — it already exists and keeps itself current.

Bing Webmaster Tools

  1. Do Google first.
  2. Sign in and choose Import from Google Search Console.
  3. Both sites come across already verified — nothing to paste into the pages at all.
  4. If that Google connection is ever revoked, Bing falls back to needing its own msvalidate.01 tag, added the same way.

Google Analytics

  1. Create a GA4 property per site — one each, not one shared, or the two elevators' traffic is mixed together.
  2. Paste the gtag snippet above </head>, same place as the verification tag.
  3. Once Analytics is live it can verify Search Console on its own, so the separate tag becomes optional.
  4. Expect the page to get a touch slower — it is the first thing on either site that would call out to another company.

Two rules if you do this

Leave the tags in forever. Both Google and Bing re-check them. Remove the tag and you quietly lose the property.

If anyone offers you the DNS method, add a record — never replace one. The mail for both domains runs on records that live in the same place, and replacing them takes the email down.

<!-- goes immediately above </head> in index.html -->
<meta name="google-site-verification" content="PASTE_WHAT_GOOGLE_GIVES_YOU">
