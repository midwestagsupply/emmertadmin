The screen is two screens

both elevators on both

Basis

  1. One row for each of the eleven delivery months, in Bids’ own order.
  2. Left of the divider is the market: the futures contract that month prices off, what it is at, and Bids’ own basis and bid.
  3. Right of it, twice: Show, Our basis, On the site — once for Badger, once for Midwest.

Hours & notices

  1. Today, the yellow bar, Monday to Friday, Saturday, Sunday.
  2. The small print under the hours, and the note under the prices.
  3. Same shape: one row per setting, one column pair per elevator.

What is true of both

  1. Both elevators are always on screen. Nothing hides one.
  2. Each has its own Save and files its own ticket. Nothing you type in one column can travel in the other one’s.
  3. A red or green outline down a column tells you which elevator you are in.

How to

the four things you will do

Set a basis, choose the months

  1. Find the month’s row. Type our basis in that elevator’s Our basis box — almost always a minus.
  2. Read across: futures plus your basis is what the site will post.
  3. Tick Show on the months you want on the site; untick one to take it off.
  4. Press Save, then the green Submit new issue. Live in about a minute.

Change today’s hours

  1. Hours & notices, the Today row.
  2. Usual uses the weekly hours below. Different uses the two boxes beside it. Closed shuts the day. Harvest uses the harvest window.
  3. Today only. It clears itself overnight, so you do not have to remember to undo it.
  4. Save and submit.

Put a notice up

  1. The Notice banner row. Write the message, set it to Show.
  2. 160 characters. It is the yellow bar under the logo on both sites.
  3. Set it back to Hide when it is no longer true. Nothing takes it down for you.
  4. Save and submit.

Break glass: type a price

  1. While the board is up this panel is not on the screen at all — with eleven live prices in front of you there is nothing to type by hand.
  2. If the feed has failed the board disappears and the panel appears in its place.
  3. Enter the number from the office. It overrides the feed until it is cleared.
  4. Clear it the moment the feed is back, or the site keeps showing your number instead of the market’s.

Blank is not zero

Blank — leave that month exactly as the site has it. It is the only thing that means that.
0 — the basis for that month is zero: we pay the contract price itself, with nothing off it. A long way above what anybody posts. If you meant “leave it alone”, clear the box.

The basis is signed, the way it is said out loud: −0.75 is seventy-five under, +0.05 is a nickel over. Almost every basis you will ever type starts with a minus.

What Save actually does

why there are two buttons

There is no password on the staff screen and no server behind it. That is deliberate — a password kept in a box somewhere is the thing that eventually leaks. The screen writes your change as a GitHub ticket instead, and GitHub decides whether you are allowed.

StepWhat happensWhat you see
1You press Save.A GitHub page opens with the whole form already written out.
2You press Submit new issue.The ticket is filed against that elevator’s site.
3A checker runs. It refuses anyone without write access and runs its own tests before touching a file.Nothing yet — about a minute.
4aAccepted. The basis, the months, the hours are written and the site rebuilds.A comment saying Done, and the ticket closes.
4bRefused. Nothing is changed.A comment naming the line that was wrong. The ticket stays open.

A closed ticket is the receipt

The ticket closing is how you know the change is live. If it is still open, read the comment — it names the line that could not be read. You do not start over: edit the ticket, fix that one line, and it runs again on its own.

If the feed goes quiet, the price comes off — on purpose

If nothing has arrived from Bids in 4 hours, both sites stop showing a number and say “Call for today’s price” instead. That is the system working. A wrong price on the page is worse than no price on the page.

It also refuses a reading where their own figures disagree with each other — a half-loaded page — and any corn price outside two to twelve dollars, which is a decimal point in the wrong place.

Questions

the ones that get asked
A farmer asks where the price comes from. What do I say?

It is that delivery month’s futures contract plus our own basis, read from Bids’ posted board automatically and rebuilt through the day. It is not a quote and it is not final until he rings.

Why does Save open a second page?

Because there is no password and no server. The second page is GitHub asking who you are. Your name and the time go on every change, nothing has to be remembered or shared, and there is no password to leak.

I pressed Save and no second page opened.

Nothing was lost. The screen says so and gives you a link to open the form by hand — use that, then press the green Submit new issue as usual. If you would rather it stopped happening, allow pop-ups for this screen.

How long until it is on the site?

About a minute after you press Submit new issue. If it is longer than five, open the ticket — it will be sitting open with a comment saying what was wrong.

The site says “Call for today’s price”. Is it broken?

No — that is the safety working. Nothing has been read from Bids in 4 hours, so rather than show a number that might be half a day old, both sites ask for a call.

I typed the wrong thing and saved it.

Save the right thing over it. Every save is a full statement of what the settings should be — the last one wins, and the site rebuilds within a minute.

Can I edit the website itself from the staff screen?

No, and that is the point. The screen sets the basis, which months are published, the hours and the notices. Everything else on the page — the price, the week table, the drying schedule — is worked out from those.

A farmer says our price is “as of” hours ago. Is it stale?

Almost always no. The board is read every ten minutes through the trading day, but the stamp is only re-set when a number actually moves. On a flat afternoon it can read six hours old while the board has been read dozens of times since.

It means “this is still the price”, not “nobody has looked”. Past 4 hours with nothing read, the site takes the price down on its own.

Does the website cost money or slow down when a lot of people visit?

No. Both sites are plain HTML files. The page does no work when somebody opens it — it fetches nothing and calculates nothing. A busy morning costs exactly the same as a quiet one.
