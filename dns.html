Pointing a domain at the site

For Kristi. What to change in GoDaddy so a domain you own opens Badger Grain Supply or Midwest Commodity Service instead of a parked page. Everything here is done in a browser. Nothing here can change a price.

Written 12 September 2026 · addresses read from GitHub's own documentation the same day

Read this bit first

2 minutes

The sites themselves are already built and already live. They sit at addresses that look like midwestagsupply.github.io/badgergrain/. What you are doing is telling a domain name you own to open one of them.

DNS is the phone book of the internet. You are editing one company's entry in it. Two things follow from that, and they are the only two things that can go wrong here:

If the domain is used for email, stop

Look for rows of type MX before you change anything. If there are any, that domain carries somebody's email. Changing the wrong row stops mail arriving, and it can take a day to notice and a day to undo.

Do not guess. Close the tab and ring Sig. He will tell you in a minute whether it is safe, and nothing is lost by asking.

The second thing: a change is not instant. Somewhere between a few minutes and a few hours, depending on how long the old answer was told to be remembered. If it has not worked after an hour, that is normal. If it has not worked the next morning, something is wrong and it is worth a call.

The three jobs

what you will actually do

Every domain needs one of these three, not all of them. Which one depends on what that domain is for. The table in the next section says which.

Job 1 — the plain domain

  1. Delete any existing A row whose Name is @.
  2. Add four new A rows, Name @, one for each address in the box below.
  3. Leave TTL on whatever GoDaddy suggests. It does not matter here.

Job 2 — the www version

  1. Find the row with Name www.
  2. Make it type CNAME pointing at midwestagsupply.github.io.
  3. If there is no www row, add one.

Job 3 — a domain that just forwards

  1. Leave DNS alone entirely.
  2. In GoDaddy, open Domain Settings and find Forwarding.
  3. Forward to the real address, permanent (301), forward only.

Job 3 is for a spare domain that should simply send people to the main one. It does not need any of Job 1 or Job 2, and doing both is how a domain ends up in a loop.

Once a domain is decided, one more thing has to happen on our side before it will work: the site has to be told to answer to that name. That is a two-minute job for Sig in the repository settings, and it has to be done for each domain. A domain pointed at us that we have not been told about shows a "404" page.

Doing it in GoDaddy

click by click

Getting to the right screen

  1. Sign in at godaddy.com.
  2. Your name, top right, then My Products.
  3. Find the domain in the list. Press DNS beside it.
  4. You are now looking at every row in that domain's phone book entry.

Before you touch anything

  1. Read down the Type column.
  2. See an MX row? Stop. Ring Sig.
  3. Otherwise, take a photo of the screen with your phone. That is your undo, and it costs five seconds.

Making the change

  1. Add New Record to add a row; the pencil to change one; the bin to delete one.
  2. Do the jobs the table calls for, then Save.
  3. GoDaddy sometimes writes the name as @ and sometimes shows the domain itself. Both mean the same thing.

How to tell it worked

Open the domain in a browser. You are looking for the grain board, the hours, and the phone number. Two things that are not failures:

What you seeWhat it meansDo
The old parked page Your computer is still remembering the old answer. Try it on your phone with wi-fi off. If the phone shows the site, it worked.
A warning about the connection not being private The security certificate is still being issued. It is automatic and it takes up to a few hours after the DNS change. Leave it. Check again after lunch.
A GitHub "404" page DNS worked. The site has not been told to answer to this name yet. Tell Sig which domain. It is a two-minute job at his end.
Nothing loads at all, next morning Something in the rows is not right. Send Sig a photo of the DNS screen.

Questions people ask

Can I break the prices doing this?

No. The prices are read and published by something else entirely. The worst that happens here is that a domain does not open the site, which is where several of them are starting from anyway.

Can I break the email doing this?

Yes, if you delete an MX row. That is the one real risk on this page, and it is why the rule at the top is to stop and ring rather than to be careful. Nothing else on the screen matters as much.

It has been ten minutes and nothing has changed.

Normal. Anywhere up to a few hours. Check on a phone with wi-fi turned off, because a phone on mobile data has not been told the old answer.

Do I need the AAAA rows?

No. They are the newer kind of address and the site works without them. Add them if you like tidiness.

Should www and the plain domain both work?

Yes, and they will, once Jobs 1 and 2 are both done. The plain one is the real address; www sends people to it.

I changed the wrong thing.

Put it back from the photo you took, then ring Sig. DNS changes are completely reversible. Nothing is lost permanently.
